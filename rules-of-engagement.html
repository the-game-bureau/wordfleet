The Word Fleet Game Notebook

Rules of Engagement

2 captains · 15–30 minutes · Ages 10+

Hide a fleet of five words on your battle sheet. Guess a whole enemy word-ship to earn a shot. Win by naming every word in the opposing fleet and exactly where it is hidden.

In this guide

  1. Get your battle sheets ready
  2. Deploy your word-ships
  3. Decide who goes first
  4. Take a turn
  5. Demand surrender

Get your battle sheets ready

Each captain tears a Battle Tracker out of the notebook or takes a printed sheet. Keep your words and launch codes hidden from your opponent. Grab a pencil and fill in the Fleet Name line.

Attack Grid
Track your shots and the letters you uncover in your opponent’s fleet.
Attack Manifest
Use these alphabet boxes to keep track of the letter tallies your opponent gives you.
Defense Grid
Hide your own word-ships here.
Defense Manifest
Look here to see how many times each letter appears in your own fleet.
Launch Codes
Use these five secret numbers to bluff for the first turn.

Deploy your word-ships

Find the five words printed above your Defense Grid. They are 5, 4, 3, 3, and 2 letters long. Each word is one ship.

  1. Pick a starting square for each word and write one letter per square.
  2. Place the letters left to right across a row or top to bottom down a column.
  3. Give each ship its own squares inside the grid. Ships may touch.

Example. Place MAP across C4, D4, and E4. Write M in C4, A in D4, and P in E4.

Decide who goes first

Either captain calls out a three-digit launch code from 000 to 999. It can be a code printed on their sheet or a bluff. The other captain chooses one response.

ResponseWhat happens
Concede The captain who called the code takes the first turn.
Challenge The caller must prove the code appears on their sheet. If it does, the caller goes first. If it was a bluff, the challenger goes first.
Counter Call an equal or higher code. You become the new caller and the other captain now chooses a response.

Keep going until someone concedes or a challenge settles who goes first.

Example. You call 850. Your opponent counters with 900. You challenge, and 900 is missing from their sheet. You take the first turn.

Take a turn

Alternate turns. At the start of your turn, choose a standard attack or demand surrender.

Guess a whole word-ship before you fire

Say one complete word you think is in your opponent’s fleet. Your opponent checks their word list.

  • Correct word. You earn a shot. Call a coordinate to fire.
  • Wrong word. Your turn ends immediately. Pass play to the other captain.

Call a coordinate

Call a column letter from A to J and a row number from 1 to 10, such as E7. Your opponent checks that square on their Defense Grid and calls a hit or a miss.

  • Miss. Put a dot in that square on your Attack Grid and pass the turn.
  • Hit. Outline the square on your Attack Grid. Take one guess at the letter in that square.

Guess one letter after a hit

  • Correct letter. Your opponent confirms it. Write the letter in the square and pass the turn.
  • Wrong letter. Your opponent checks their Defense Manifest and tells you how many times the letter you guessed appears across their entire fleet, including letters already found. Record the tally and pass the turn.

You can fire at a square you hit before on a later turn to try a different letter.

Example. You correctly guess TIDE and earn a shot. You hit E4 and guess A. The square holds P, and A appears twice in the opposing fleet. Your opponent says “A tally 2.” Mark the hit, record the tally, and pass the turn.

Demand surrender

At the start of your turn, you can demand surrender in place of attacking. Say all five enemy words, their spellings, and the first and last coordinates of each word.

Your opponent waits for you to finish calling the whole fleet, then checks every word and location against their Defense Grid.

  • Everything is right. You win immediately.
  • Any word, spelling, or location is wrong. Your opponent wins immediately.

Example. Call “MAP, M-A-P, from C4 to E4,” then give the same information for the other four word-ships.
